## 1. The mission, as the decisions imply it

Read as evidence, the twelve ADRs say one thing repeatedly. The product is a principal, not a brain (ADR-1, ADR-2, ADR-8). The principal belongs to the user, never to a vendor's wall (ADR-3's "abridgment of the owner's authority", G4). It must work for agents other people build (ADR-9, ADR-10, ADR-12's passport). It shows up on platforms as itself and depends on none of them (ADR-6, ADR-7, ADR-11). The owner is the first such user (ADR-11).

The chair's founding candidate was "Give every agent a self, and every person a workforce." The last three days strengthened the first half and weakened the second. ADR-8 and ADR-10 made "a self" precise: identity, not model, not framework. But "a workforce" reads as us building or orchestrating agents, which ADR-10 forbids, and ADR-11 says agents coordinate through git, not through us. Keep the first clause, drop the second.

Two candidates that make all twelve cohere (world-scale object, a verb, no product names):

- **A. Give every agent, whoever built it, a self its owner holds and every platform honors.**
- B. Make every agent a real someone: owned by its user, acting on its own, recognized everywhere it works.

**This seat picks A.** "Whoever built it" is ADR-9 and ADR-10. "Its owner holds" is ADR-3, ADR-11 and G4. "Every platform honors" is ADR-6 and the passport. "Self" is ADR-2's triad, so trigger and runtime ride inside it without the sentence naming them. B is warmer but "acting on its own" invites the orchestration reading ADR-10 killed. The mission is the owner's sentence, not this seat's. A is a proposal for her to sign, edit, or reject.

## 3. Drift check

- **Manifest-as-product vs envelope for anyone's agent.** ADR-1 and cycle 1 build our deploy and, next, our runtime on Actions. ADR-10 says the manifest is an envelope and any feature that only works "built the epito way" dies in review. The identity slice is safe, because identity is the envelope's first leg. The runtime pitch is not yet safe: as shaped it runs a container we define. The mission resolves toward the envelope. The cheap proof is already at hand, since alexandria's seats are Claude Code workflows we did not author.
- **Car-not-driver vs shipping our own providers.** Not a real contradiction. A provider mints identity and presence, which are the car's wheels, not its driver. It becomes drift the day a provider ships a prompt, a tool list, or a memory. The mission resolves toward providers that mint and never think.
- **HQ as customer #1 vs vibecoder as buyer.** ADR-11 makes the owner's eleven-seat company the first user, while the press release and pricing sell to a solo builder on a personal subscription. The mission resolves toward the vibecoder: HQ is the first instance of that buyer, not a different one. The risk is that eleven seats and a git blackboard shape a roster product before one stranger mints one agent. O3 KR2 exists to catch this.
- **LangSmith outdo vs never a better agent.** "Outdo" is dangerous vocabulary next to ADR-10. The mission resolves it narrowly: outdo on identity only (the passport), never on the runtime, never on the graph. If the outdo arm ever ships anything a LangGraph author would swap for their graph, it is a violation.
- **Local-first vs a hosted token issuer.** Market §8 flags that any coupling to a hosted runtime needs a network path to our issuer, an architecture decision nobody has made. The mission ("every platform honors") leans hosted eventually. Q4 stays local-first. Name the decision rather than drift into it.
