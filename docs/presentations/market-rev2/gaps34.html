6 · Gaps G3 and G4, the priority item

Audit by construction; the portable principal nobody sells.

G3 · Per-agent audit as the compliance floor

Evidence: EU AI Act high-risk obligations fully enforced since 2026-08-02; Article 12 requires logs sufficient to reconstruct what an agent did. The market priced it: Oasis $120M, NewCore $66M, Oak $60M, GitGuardian $50M — $392M announced around RSAC alone.

Our contribution: attribution that cannot be retrofitted. A distinct principal means every commit and call lands under the agent's name on the platform itself; an owner-token agent can never produce that trail, no matter what logging is bolted on.

Not solved: we are the substrate, not the GRC product. Dashboards and conformity assessment are Oasis's and Entra's business (see trap T3).

G4 · The whitespace (priority)

Assemble the universe table's identity column and look once: Microsoft mints into Entra, AWS into AgentCore, Google into SPIFFE, Slack into its apps, Notion and Asana into their workspaces. Every primitive real; every one stops at its vendor's wall. Nobody issues the agent an identity the user owns, that follows it from GitHub to Slack to its own database, minted from one manifest. The enterprise near-equivalent starts at $15/user/month inside Microsoft's estate. The indie version does not exist.

The hedge, said plainly: this is whitespace partly because the giants' incentives point away from it — and their incentives could change. Law 1 (never a dependency) is the only structural protection.
