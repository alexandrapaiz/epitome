5 · Gaps G1 and G2

No middleman vaults; the resident agent, done safely.

G1 · Cross-SaaS autonomy without master keys

Evidence: Zapier fronts 9,000+ apps through one managed-credential vault, by its own description. Only 8.5% of MCP servers in the wild use OAuth at all (Astrix, via secondary source, flagged); over-scoped tokens are the most common misconfiguration; Cloudflare shipped optional OAuth scopes naming MCP agents "requesting the union of everything they might do."

Our contribution: no vault in the middle. Each agent's principal is minted on each platform, scoped in the manifest, revocable where it lives. Blast radius of one compromise: that agent's scopes, nothing else.

Not solved: platform scope coarseness is theirs, not ours; prompt injection can misuse scopes an agent legitimately holds.

G2 · The resident local agent, done safely

Evidence: the OpenClaw crisis — 135,000+ stars in weeks, then 42,000 instances exposed with 93% showing critical auth flaws, plaintext keys, a CVSS 8.8 one-click RCE, and 12% of its skill registry confirmed malicious (341 of 2,857). People want resident agents badly enough to run this.

Our contribution: exactly the three things OpenClaw lacked — per-agent containment (rootless podman), secrets out of plaintext (OS keychain), identity that is not the owner's (a stolen credential is one agent's scopes for at most an hour).

Not solved: prompt injection, a malicious skill the user installs, an unpatched host, the closed laptop. Say all four out loud in the docs.
